# MEMORANDUM (FICTIONAL — sample material for simulation testing)

**To:** All Faculty and Staff
**From:** Office of the Provost
**Re:** Proposed Institutional Guidelines on Artificial Intelligence — Request for Comment
**Date:** September 2, 2026

The university proposes the following guidelines for the use of generative
artificial intelligence, effective spring semester. Comments are requested
within 30 days.

## 1. Instruction and coursework

1.1 Each instructor retains authority over AI use in their courses, but every
syllabus must include one of three standard AI-use statements (Prohibited,
Permitted with Disclosure, or Required/Integrated) beginning in spring.

1.2 The university will discontinue its license for AI-detection software at
the end of the fall term, citing accuracy and equity concerns. Suspected
academic-integrity cases will follow the existing honor process without
detector evidence.

1.3 A new one-credit AI Literacy module will become a general-education
requirement for incoming students beginning next fall. Departments are invited
to propose discipline-specific sections; a stipend of $1,500 per developed
section is available for the first ten approved.

## 2. Approved tools and data

2.1 A single enterprise AI platform will be procured and made available to all
students, faculty, and staff at no cost. Only the enterprise platform may be
used with student records, advising notes, or any data covered by FERPA.

2.2 Use of personal or free-tier AI accounts for university business involving
student data is prohibited beginning immediately.

2.3 Department purchases of AI tools or AI add-ons must pass a security and
accessibility review before contract signature.

## 3. Staff and operations

3.1 Divisions are asked to identify, by March 1, three routine processes
suitable for AI-assisted automation. Efficiency gains are intended to be
reinvested in service quality; the memo notes that "no reduction in force is
planned in connection with this initiative."

3.2 An AI use-in-hiring moratorium: no AI screening of employment applications
until a fairness review is complete.

## 4. Support and governance

4.1 A standing AI Governance Council will be formed: six faculty (elected by
the faculty senate), three staff (appointed by division heads), two students,
and three administrators. The Council reviews tool approvals, policy
exceptions, and an annual public report.

4.2 The Center for Teaching Excellence and Digital Learning Support will
receive one new position between them to support course redesign. Faculty may
apply for $2,000 summer redesign grants (twenty available).

4.3 No additional base funding is allocated to departments in connection with
these guidelines in the current biennium.

Comments may be directed to the Provost's office or raised through the faculty
senate and staff advisory council.
